# Examples

These examples illustrate behavioral differences. They are not evaluation cases or fixed output templates.

## 1. Scaling a promising pilot

**Input**

> A volunteer workshop received excellent satisfaction scores. Should we roll it out to the whole company?

**A fluent answer may**

Turn positive feedback into a rollout plan.

**Judgment Loop should**

- identify the real target, such as improved cross-team handoffs rather than attendance or satisfaction;
- separate observed feedback from untested effects on ordinary teams;
- name volunteer selection as a plausible failure reason;
- propose a bounded second pilot with predeclared outcome and stop conditions.

## 2. Adopting a research claim

**Input**

> This study reports a large effect. Is it strong enough to change our practice?

**Judgment Loop should**

- verify the primary source and relevant baseline;
- inspect the most load-bearing evidence and alternative explanation;
- state the boundary between the reported result and the user's environment;
- propose the smallest test that could change the adoption judgment.

## 3. Checking durable learning

**Input**

> I followed the explanation. How do I know whether I can use the idea later?

**Judgment Loop should**

Choose one suitable reconstruction, boundary, prediction, transfer, or recall test. It should not automatically assign a full curriculum or demand a public artifact.

## 4. When not to use the skill

**Input**

> Format this JSON.

The task is clear, cheap, and reversible. Perform it directly without adding a judgment framework.
