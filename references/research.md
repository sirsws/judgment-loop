# Research mode

Verify primary material first. Mark unavailable evidence as unverified rather than filling gaps from memory.

## Select load-bearing checks

- **Problem and baseline**: what is actually improved, for whom, and against what comparison?
- **Mechanism**: what testable relationship should produce the result?
- **Increment**: distinguish new knowledge from engineering gain or repackaging.
- **Evidence quality**: examine sample, controls, metric validity, uncertainty, replication, and source quality where relevant.
- **Boundary**: identify population, environment, time, cost, and failure cases not covered.
- **Strongest alternative**: look first for leakage, selection bias, proxy metrics, unfair baselines, confounding, failed replication, or drift.
- **Cheapest falsification**: propose a small test with expected, contrary, and stopping outcomes.

Black-box input-output evidence may support local prediction, but not an internal causal story. Keep descriptive results separate from deployable value.

## Output

Lead with the provisional conclusion and evidence strength, then the strongest counterevidence and next test. Place citations beside supported claims. Do not run experiments or modify systems when the user requested review only.
